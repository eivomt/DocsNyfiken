Scattering

One of the more counterintuitive predictions of quantum mechanics is that particles can be detected in regions that would be classically inaccessible. A particle approaching a potential barrier with insufficient energy would, according to classical mechanics, always be reflected. In quantum mechanics, however, there remains a finite probability that the particle will be transmitted through the barrier.

This phenomenon, known as quantum tunneling, arises because the wave function does not vanish abruptly at the boundary of a classically forbidden region. Instead, it decays exponentially inside the barrier. If the barrier is of finite width, a non-zero portion of the wave function may emerge on the opposite side, giving rise to a measurable transmission probability.

Quantum tunneling is not merely a theoretical curiosity. It plays a central role in numerous physical processes and technologies, including nuclear decay, scanning tunneling microscopy, and modern semiconductor devices. As transistor dimensions continue to shrink, tunneling currents increasingly limit further miniaturization, presenting one of the challenges associated with extending Moore's Law.

The visualization presented here illustrates the scattering of a wave packet from a finite potential barrier. Users may vary the barrier height and the initial mean momentum of the packet, observing how these parameters influence the reflected and transmitted components. An optional momentum-space view can also be enabled to show the corresponding evolution of the momentum distribution.

For performance reasons, the visualization itself consists of a collection of precomputed animations. The interface selects and displays the animation corresponding to the chosen parameters. The underlying numerical calculations used to generate these animations are performed separately in Python.
