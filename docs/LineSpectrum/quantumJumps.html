Quantum jumps

An undisturbed hydrogen atom will in theory stay in whatever state it's in forever. But if it's interacted with, it's state may change, either by receiving / absorbing energy thereby transitioning to a higher energy state, or by giving off energy (by firing a photon), transitioning to a lower energy state. When a hydrogen atom transitions from a higher to a lower energy state, the energy released is a precise quantity equal to:
E_\gamma = E_i - E_f = -13.6eV(\frac{1}{n_i^2} - \frac{1}{n_f^2})
We can convert the energy E_\gamma to a wavelength according to the Planck formula, which says the energy of a photon is proportional to its frequency:
E_\gamma = hv
for electromagnetic waves, their speed is the speed of light and so the wavelength is: \lambda = \frac{c}{v}
inserting this into our equation for E_\gamma we get:
\lambda = \frac{ch}{E_\gamma}

So the possible wavelengths a hydrogen atom can emit are strictly the wavelengths that satisfy this equality. Although the possible wavelengths vary, the basic sentiment of quantized energies is true for all atoms, thereby giving us the fingerprints of the elements.
